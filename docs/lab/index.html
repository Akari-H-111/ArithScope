Can an attention head reliably look every P tokens back, at any context length? This page computes, exactly and live, the number theory behind ArithScope: a zero-initialized Ramanujan-sum bias added to each attention head.

Lemma L2 · the comb gap

One order, one exact comb

Each kernel K_m equals 1 exactly on multiples of m and is at most ½ everywhere else. It is a table lookup on n mod m in integer arithmetic, so the comb never drifts, whether at offset 10 or offset 1015.

Theorem T3 · optimality

The best possible periodic attention bias

Among all positive-definite P-periodic biases with unit diagonal, the largest gap between the comb and everything else is P/(P−1). Only one function reaches it: the convex Ramanujan mixture Σ φ(d)/(P−1)·K_d over divisors d > 1 of P. The natural Fourier control with the same periods and the same parameter count falls far short whenever P is prime.

Theorem T4 and Proposition P1 · identifiability

Which periods can a bank of orders see?

A head with orders M can make exactly the combs lcm(S)·ℤ, S ⊆ M, its strict argmax. Offsets with the same kernel signature are indistinguishable to every such head. Pick a bank and a target period: what matters is the lcm, not the number of orders.

Theorem T5 · why exact periods are necessary

RoPE cannot hold a comb forever

RoPE frequencies b^(−2k/d) are algebraic numbers, so by Lindemann's theorem none of their integer combinations is a nonzero rational multiple of 2π. Weyl equidistribution then forces the attention mass of any fixed RoPE head on a residue class mod P toward the uniform share 1/P as the context grows. Learned real frequencies drift the same way at about 1/|ε| tokens.
